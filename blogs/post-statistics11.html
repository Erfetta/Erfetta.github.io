Simulating a Wiener Process (Brownian Motion) Using the Euler–Maruyama Method

The Wiener process, also known as Brownian motion, is one of the most fundamental stochastic processes in applied mathematics. It plays a central role in physics, finance, biology, neuroscience, signal processing, probability theory, and many mathematical models involving randomness.

Brownian motion was first observed by Robert Brown in 1827 and later formalized mathematically by Norbert Wiener in the 20th century. Its mathematical structure forms the basis of stochastic calculus and stochastic differential equations (SDEs).

In this homework we use the Euler–Maruyama method—developed in a previous assignment—to numerically simulate a Wiener process, and optionally a general SDE of the form: \[ dX_t = a(X_t,t)\,dt + b(X_t,t)\,dW_t. \]

🌊 What Is a Wiener Process?

Formally, a Wiener process \(W_t\) is a stochastic process satisfying:

  • \(W_0 = 0\)
  • Independent increments
  • Gaussian increments: \(W_{t+s} - W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, s)\)
  • Continuous paths (but almost surely nowhere differentiable)

Intuitively, at every instant the process is “kicked” by a small independent Gaussian shock, producing an irregular, jittery trajectory.

Connection with SDEs

The Wiener process formally satisfies the SDE: \[ dW_t = dW_t, \] i.e., \[ a(x,t) = 0, \quad b(x,t) = 1. \]

📐 The Euler–Maruyama Method

For a general SDE: \[ dX_t = a(X_t,t)\,dt + b(X_t,t)\,dW_t, \] the Euler–Maruyama approximation is: \[ X_{t+\Delta t} = X_t + a(X_t,t)\,\Delta t + b(X_t,t)\,\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_t, \] where \(Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\).

Special case: Wiener process

Setting \(a = 0\) and \(b = 1\) gives: \[ W_{t+\Delta t} = W_t + \sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_t. \] This is exactly the scheme implemented in the simulator below.

🌍 Applications of the Wiener Process

  • Physics: diffusion, heat equation, particle movement
  • Finance: Black–Scholes model for option pricing
  • Neuroscience: neuron firing models
  • Biology: molecular motion in fluids
  • Engineering: modelling noise in signals

The Wiener process is the fundamental “noise source” in stochastic modelling. Many SDEs describe real-world phenomena by adding drift (systematic behavior) and diffusion (random fluctuations) to a Wiener process.
